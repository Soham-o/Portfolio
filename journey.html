The Road to Building AI Systems.

The Story So Far

  • Early Curiosity — Before College

    The Beginning

    I didn't fall in love with coding because of algorithms or theory. I fell in love with it the first time I wrote something that replaced a task I was doing manually. That moment — realising a computer could just do this for me — was a pivot I couldn't un-see. Everything after was chasing the same feeling: find the repetitive thing, eliminate it, move on.

  • Discovering AI — 2023

    From "How Does This Work?" to "What Can I Build?"

    Started with the standard path — linear regression, decision trees, the ML textbook. But when LLMs arrived and I could describe something in plain English and it just worked, the question shifted completely. Not "how does this function?" but "what can I eliminate with this?" That shift from learner to builder is when things actually got interesting.

  • Builder Phase — 2024

    The Internship Search That Became a Pipeline

    I was spending hours daily searching for internships across five platforms, deduplicating manually, and still missing good opportunities. One evening I thought: this is just a pattern — it's a solvable problem. Three weeks later, InternPilot AI was running and I deleted all those bookmarks. That was the moment I committed to the builder identity over the student one.

  • Going Deeper — 2024–2025

    PVCA: When AI Answers Your Phone

    PVCA 1.0 started as a half-serious idea: "what if my phone had an AI receptionist?" Six days later it was live — Twilio forwarding missed calls to a Dograh AI voice agent, which collected caller info and pushed a Telegram summary in under 15 seconds. The constraint (Twilio's 5-second webhook timeout) required async-first design and made the engineering sharper. Constraints are features.

  • Current Phase — 2025

    CS Student by Day. AI Systems Builder by Night.

    Currently in my second year at SGTU studying Computer Science, building autonomous pipelines on the side. Selected for the Microsoft Elevate AI program. Internships at Unified Mentor (data analysis) and Edunet Foundation (frontend development) grounded what I was learning in real delivery expectations. The dissertation is next — so is whatever workflow annoys me this month.

  • Future Vision — What's Next

    Agents That Reason, Not Just Execute

    The next chapter is more sophisticated — multi-step reasoning, persistent memory, tool use, real-world integrations that handle edge cases gracefully. I want to build systems that don't just automate tasks but make intelligent decisions in context. The goal isn't automation for its own sake. It's giving people back hours they're spending on things that shouldn't require a human.

THINGS I ENJOY

Exploring new AI tools before they're mainstream. Turning "what if I automated this?" into a weekend project. Reading about agentic AI and multi-model reasoning. Finding the laziest — most elegant — solution to any problem. Explaining AI to people who assumed it was magic.

I have a running list of "repetitive things that should be automated." It is always longer than my project backlog. Always.

HOW I WORK

Prototype fast, then engineer properly. README-first: if I can't explain it, I don't fully understand it. Async and non-blocking wherever possible. Late-night debugging is often the most productive time.

My benchmark for a good system: if it breaks silently at 3 AM and nobody notices until morning, it was working correctly.

"I'm still early in this journey — but every system I ship is another proof that intelligent automation doesn't need a budget, a team, or a deadline. It needs a problem worth solving and the stubbornness to see it through."
