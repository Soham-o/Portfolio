The Story So Far

  • Starting Out

    Learning Through Projects

    I became interested in programming by exploring how software could handle repetitive tasks. That curiosity continues to influence the problems I choose to work on and the solutions I try to build.

  • Exploring AI

    Learning Through Experiments

    I explored machine-learning ideas and large-language-model tools, then began applying what I learned through projects. I'm interested in understanding where these tools help and where their limitations matter.

  • Building Automation Projects

    From Problem to Project

    InternPilot AI explores the process of collecting internship listings, removing duplicates, organizing opportunities, and presenting results in a digest. The focus here is the workflow, not an unverified claim about time saved or continuous operation.

  • Voice AI Experiments

    PVCA · Workflow Project

    PVCA explores a missed-call workflow using Twilio, a voice agent, webhook processing, and Telegram summaries. It is presented here as a project, without unverified claims about response time, operating cost, or production availability.

  • Current Focus

    B.Tech · Computer Science at SGTU

    I'm pursuing a B.Tech in Computer Science at SGTU. My public projects include machine-learning notebooks, data-analysis work, web applications, and experiments with AI and automation. The project pages link to public repositories and README documentation where available.

  • Future Vision

    Agents That Reason, Not Just Execute

    The next chapter is more sophisticated — multi-step reasoning, persistent memory, tool use, real-world integrations that handle edge cases gracefully. I want to build systems that don't just automate tasks but make intelligent decisions in context. The goal isn't automation for its own sake. It's giving people back hours they're spending on things that shouldn't require a human.

What Guides the Work

Build for real problems, not demos
$0 infra sharpens design
Self-running systems are worth building twice
Failed experiments = research
Reliability over features
Automation gives time back
README-first development
Async and non-blocking by default

THINGS I ENJOY

Exploring new AI tools before they're mainstream. Turning "what if I automated this?" into a weekend project. Reading about agentic AI and multi-model reasoning. Finding the laziest — most elegant — solution to any problem. Explaining AI to people who assumed it was magic.

I have a running list of "repetitive things that should be automated." It is always longer than my project backlog. Always.

HOW I WORK

Prototype fast, then engineer properly. README-first: if I can't explain it, I don't fully understand it. Async and non-blocking wherever possible. Late-night debugging is often the most productive time.

My benchmark for a good system: if it breaks silently at 3 AM and nobody notices until morning, it was working correctly.

"I'm still early in this journey — but every system I ship is another proof that intelligent automation doesn't need a budget, a team, or a deadline. It needs a problem worth solving and the stubbornness to see it through."
